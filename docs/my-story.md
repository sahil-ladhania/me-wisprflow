# Sahil's Story

> Context file. Written from Sahil's own account, in order.
> Rules for anything that uses this file:
> - Never invent or inflate facts.
> - The breakup: talk only about Sahil's pain and growth. No details of how it ended, no blame, nothing about the other person.

## 0. Basics
- Sahil, 25. Lives in Bangalore. Originally from Patna, Bihar.
- BBA, Christ University Bangalore (2021–24). Bachelor's in CS, IGNOU.
- Full-stack / AI engineer (MERN, AI products). Works best where engineering, product and growth meet.
- Three things that shaped him: dance, food and cooking, tech and product.

## 1. School: dance
- Danced a lot as a teenager.
- Was the cultural head of his school.
- Choreographed performances for school events.
- Taught seniors, juniors and his own batchmates.
- Everyone in school knew him. He was a bit famous there for this.

## 2. School: cooking
- Always knew he loved cooking and was decently good at it.
- In Class 9 his school held a culinary competition.
- About 95–96% of participants were girls. Only 2–3 boys took part.
- He came first. It was a very basic dish, but everyone loved it.
- He still has the certificate.
- Friends and teachers were curious: "Sahil, you dance, you cook. What else can you do?"
- He was very happy and told his mom.
- The more he cooked, the clearer it got: he wanted to become a chef.

## 3. School: relationship and studies
- A relationship started in Class 9. It got serious.
- He wasn't interested in studies at all. He was an average student.
- He was the centre of his school's cultural life and liked that attention.

## 4. After Class 10: choosing a stream
- He had to choose science, commerce or arts.
- His only dreams were opening a restaurant or becoming a dancer. He had no other plan.
- What he noticed about his classmates:
  - some chose because their parents told them to
  - some chose science because everyone else did
  - some chose science because they scored well in 10th
  - some chose commerce to avoid studying hard
  - very few chose because they truly wanted to become something
- His nature: "If I like something, I do it. If I don't, I don't."
- He connects this to his ADHD. He used to wonder if he was the dumb one. Now he understands how his brain works, its strengths and weaknesses.

## 5. Choosing science (an honest mistake)
- He chose science, but not for engineering. He knew nothing about engineering streams or what an IIT paper looked like.
- The real reason, which he denied even to himself at first: his girlfriend chose science, and he wanted to stay close and maybe be in the same section.
- He told his parents he wanted to become an engineer and crack IIT, so they wouldn't ask questions.
- His parents never forced him into anything.

## 6. The bike
- His father promised him a bike if he scored 90% in Class 10.
- He scored around 80%. No bike. He was very upset.
- Then one day his father bought him a Royal Enfield anyway.
- He still has it. He calls it his "second love".
- Riding gives him mental peace. Life and work are busy; a ride is his relief. It's a huge part of his life.
- His dream: ride the best roads in the world, not only in India, on his own bike.

## 7. Class 11–12: the best two years
- Called them the best two years of his life.
- Met his best friend in 2018 (9 years ago now). They became a trio with one more friend.
- Kept dancing and performing.
- For coaching, he joined one in his own locality. She came to his locality to join the same coaching.

## 8. After Class 12: the chef conversation
- He never prepared for IIT and never wanted to.
- He told his parents he didn't want to do it.
- On the terrace his father asked, "What do you actually want to become?"
- He said, "Papa, I want to become a chef and open a restaurant."
- The two options:
  | Option | Cost |
  |---|---|
  | Culinary / hospitality degree, work with big brands | ₹20–25 lakh |
  | Generic management degree | ₹10–12 lakh |
- His father never mentioned money. He only asked, "Do you really want to do it? If you do, go. I'll manage. If you're confused, don't."
- Deep down he wanted it.
- But he did the maths. A middle-class family. He is the elder son, and his younger brother's education was coming next. He didn't want to become a burden.
- He gave up the culinary path and chose management at Christ University. He calls it a sacrifice.

## 9. College, year 1: the pasta stall and the cafe offer
- He needs a mission to survive. He can't sit idle for long.
- A college fest let students set up stalls and cook and sell. He took the chance.
- Sold pasta worth ₹20,000 in 4–5 hours. People loved it.
- He cooked a lot at his PG. The owner gave him a separate room with a proper kitchen.
- He cooked for the PG owner, a wealthy man, who was very impressed.
- The owner asked what he wanted to become. Sahil said a chef with his own restaurant.
- A few weeks later the owner offered a business: he'd fund a cafe, 50/50 profit, and Sahil would own everything: design, menu, team, operations.
- Sahil was thrilled. It was the mission he'd always wanted.
- They ran weekly menu-tasting sessions for about a month. No paperwork yet.
- He asked his father. His father said: "You are there to study. You won't manage the time."
- He was upset and confused. In the end he cancelled and apologised to the owner.
- Back to square one: what now?

## 10. College: searching for direction
- Tried finance and accounting. Coming from science, it took time, and he didn't like the theory. Stopped.
- Tried marketing. Didn't like the theory either, but loved the actual work a marketer does.
- Chose marketing as his specialisation in third year.

## 11. Year 2: discovering code
- In second year he wanted something real to spend his time on. He wasn't enjoying life or work.
- His brother lived in Delhi and worked at Zomato. Sahil visited on weekends.
- His brother worked on a black terminal, running code. It looked cool. Sahil kept asking, "What is this? What is that?"
- His interest grew.
- In the winter vacation he went home and researched how to learn programming. Started Python.
- He told his school best friend, who had taken engineering (BTech) and dreamed of Google and Microsoft.
- By then his friend had come to hate coding. He was shocked: "Bro, what are you even saying?"
- His friend still guided him: "Start with Python. Solve pattern problems."
- It was hard at first. But solving problems gave him a kind of peace.
- His brother also guided him: what to learn next.
- He kept learning and building through college.

## 12. The 2am Zomato moment (2021)
- One night at 2am he was very hungry and ordered on Zomato. Zomato was booming then.
- The food came. He ate it sitting on his bed.
- The satisfaction came from eating it, not just from the delivery.
- He thought: "What kind of technology is this? You open an app and food comes to your door."
- His realisation: you can build something that gives this feeling to thousands of people.
- It became his dream to work at Zomato as an engineer.
- He knew Zomato hired from top colleges and wouldn't even give him an interview without an engineering degree.

## 13. After graduating
- Freelanced and earned a decent amount. Didn't join any company.
- Learned Data Structures and Algorithms for 6 months. Got decent at it.
- The opportunities he got weren't great.
- He has a fear of becoming average.
- His belief: "Either I need it all or I don't need it at all."
- He wants the best, and knows you have to become the person who deserves it. He is ready to.
- So he decided to start his own thing.

## 14. The Zomato cold email (July 2026)
- When: July 2026, after the rebuild, so on the page it's Stop 11, between the rebuild and Wispr Flow. The email went out on Jul 5 and HR replied on Jul 28 (Gmail screenshots in assets/media/scans/).
- It was spontaneous: one random midnight he thought "why not?" and sent it. He didn't expect anything; he was shooting for the stars, whoever cared.
- He cold-emailed Deepinder Goyal (Zomato's founder): he's a curious child too, he doesn't have an engineering background, he taught himself, and he wants to work with the team.
- On Jul 28 Zomato HR wrote back: he'd been shortlisted to interview for the engineering team. They asked which role he preferred.
- It was a big confidence boost: a dream come true. He was proud of it then and still is.
- He didn’t take the interview. He was busy running his firm (Zyntohouse), then fell sick, and it got rescheduled. Not arrogance.

## 15. The lowest point (June 2025)
- In June 2025 his relationship of about 8 years ended. She was the person he'd been with since Class 9.
- He won't share how it ended or who was at fault.
- It went very badly for him. One of the worst things that has happened in his life.
- His confidence was gone. He was at his deepest bottom. He didn't want to live anymore.
- Two months later, in August 2025, he started Zyntohouse.
- Instead of going in a negative direction, he chose a positive one. He's proud of that.
- What came out of it:
  - He became much closer to his father, his parents, his brother and his friends.
  - He started taking life less seriously. Nothing is as serious as we think. Do what you really love.
  - No regrets. At first he regretted things. Now he doesn't. Every step was him.
- His view on individuality:
  - It doesn't come from a college or a course. Many people can have those.
  - It comes from your experiences: the wars you fought, the fears you beat, the darkest times when no one was there.
  - Nobody else has his experiences, and that is what makes him unique.
- His curiosity about people grew. He realised he didn't truly know someone he'd been with for 8 years. That made him want to understand people and life more deeply.
- He says he's a changed man now.

## 16. Zyntohouse
- A two-person tech agency for Indian SMBs and non-tech founders, co-founded with Harshit.
- Running for about 1.5 years.
- Started August 2025, still running. On the page it's kept small.

## 17. lulu: the voice-first dating app
- Origin: after the breakup he had her voice notes. He listened to them for hours.
- In those voice notes he felt she was right beside him. He could feel every emotion and imagine her expressions.
- Insight: voice is powerful because it adds a human touch. It carries emotion that text can't.
- Voice AI was booming. He went deep into how voice agents are built.
- His question: why do people choose each other? Looks? Education? Money?
- He installed every dating app (Hinge, Tinder, Wavelength and more) to study what emotions they trigger and what works.
- Thesis: people swipe on looks. They read prompts only after they like the photo. If they don't like the photo, they never scroll down.
- Being less attractive doesn't mean someone isn't a good, fun person or a good partner.
- So he built lulu to close that gap: personality first, through voice.
- Shipped a waitlist page. Grew it through his own LinkedIn content from 0 to 3,500+ signups, 37% of them women.
- Personally spoke to about 200 people on the waitlist.
- Built a working prototype and demoed it on calls.
- After about 6 months the signals weren't truly positive. People said "nice idea, very unique", but he felt they weren't being honest. Users say they want personality-first, but they still swipe on looks.
- He also didn't want the app to attract people for the wrong reason, like "I don't look good, so this app is for me".
- He worked on lulu from April to September 2026: a six-month experiment.
- He paused lulu in September 2026.
- What he learned about himself: he loves product and growth. He thrives at the intersection of product, growth and engineering.

## 18. Wispr Flow

- He dictated most of this site's story with Wispr Flow.
- After Zomato, Wispr Flow is the second product that gave him that same feeling of satisfaction.
- Has cold-emailed Tanay (Wispr Flow's co-founder) 3–4 times. No reply.
- He watched a video where Tanay's father talks about Tanay being a very curious child.
- Sahil wants that feeling: his father speaking about him that way when he builds something good.
- He commented on that video. Tanay replied: "That's an amazing goal to have, keep grinding and you'll get there!" (exact words, from the screenshot in assets/media/scans/tanay-reply.jpg)
- He'd join in any role, even as a generalist. He believes in the mission.
- He's mission-driven. Without a mission or goal he gets frustrated and anxious.
- He doesn't care how many hours it takes.

## 19. Who he is
- Not an introvert or extrovert. He speaks less, but when it's needed he speaks, and speaks well.
- Doesn't talk just to feel relevant.
- Confident, not arrogant. Knows who he is and what he can pull off. Doesn't need anyone to tell him he can or can't.
